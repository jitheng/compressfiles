Why are PDF files so large?

PDFs are large primarily because of embedded images. A single full-resolution photograph can add 2–5MB to a document. Scanned pages are especially large — a 300 DPI scan of one A4 page can be 1–2MB before any compression. Our tool re-encodes those images at a lower JPEG quality, often reducing a 10MB PDF to under 1MB.

How much can file size be reduced?

It depends on the content. Image-heavy PDFs (scanned documents, brochures, presentations) typically compress by 80–90%. Text-only documents (reports, contracts) may already be small and compress by 40–60%. A 20MB scanned document can routinely compress to under 2MB.
